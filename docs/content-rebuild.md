2. **First Uniform Bar Examination expands pathway to practice in Wisconsin**
   - Date: 2026-07-29 17:50:55
   - Summary: “Wisconsin’s first administration of the Uniform Bar Examination expands options for law graduates seeking admission to practice in the state.”
   - Hero image: **testing-bar-exam-students-desks-school-college-study-1200x630.jpg**
   - Body:

     The Wisconsin Supreme Court on Tuesday, March 24, adopted the Uniform Bar Examination (UBE), which provides the ability to transfer UBE scores from tests taken in other states.

     The order, [2026 WI 9](https://www.wicourts.gov/sc/scord/DisplayDocument.pdf?content=pdf&seqNo=1096782), marks the first enactment of a proposal from the Supreme Court’s [Attorney Recruitment and Retention Committee Report and Recommendations](https://www.wicourts.gov/publications/reports/docs/arrcreport25.pdf) released in November.

     “Wisconsin is facing real challenges in recruiting and retaining qualified attorneys,” said Chief Justice Jill J. Karofsky. “This change reduces barriers to practice while maintaining the standards our courts and communities expect.

     “It is a practical step that helps connect more attorneys with the people who need them.”

     The order changes parts of Supreme Court Rule (SCR) [chapter 40](https://www.wicourts.gov/sc/scrule/DisplayDocument.pdf?content=pdf&seqNo=1096840) governing admission to the bar, effective July 1, 2026.

     The first bar examination in Wisconsin using the UBE will take place in July.

3. **Wisconsin Court System pilots online portal for domestic abuse restraining order petitions**
   - Date: 2026-07-22 20:54:38
   - Summary: “A new pilot program allows eligible users to prepare and electronically file domestic abuse restraining order petitions through an online portal.”
   - Featured image: **0726roportal400.jpg**
   - Body:

     The Wisconsin Court System has begun piloting a new online, mobile-first portal designed to make it easier for people to complete and file domestic abuse temporary restraining order petitions.

     The plain-language portal allows petitioners to create or log in to an eFiling account, generate standard court forms, and submit those forms to the circuit court in one guided process. The portal is designed to reduce confusion for petitioners, help them provide complete information, and make the filing process easier to navigate.

     The pilot is underway in eight counties: Adams, Barron, Brown, Dane, Kenosha, Milwaukee, Sauk, and Waukesha. CCAP and Court Operations staff are providing live, onsite training and technical assistance to participating counties before the portal is made available statewide.

     In Adams County, the July 9 launch included an educational presentation for the county’s Coordinated Community Response Team, which brings together local partners working on issues related to domestic violence and sexual assault. Kelsey Mullins of Court Operations co-presented on the portal with Adams County Clerk of Court Miranda Christensen, while Adams County Circuit Court Judge Tania M. Bonnett presented on local injunction processes and procedures. Then, Kelsey Mullins and CCAP representatives John Illgen and Ann Oberle presented to Adams County Circuit Court stakeholders and advocates to walk through the portal experience for petitioners and impacts on court processes.

     During the pilot, the portal is accessible at participating courthouses and partner locations, including victim advocate agencies. That approach is meant to connect petitioners with available assistance from trained advocates, clerks, or attorneys as they navigate the new process.

     “People seeking a restraining order are often coming to the court system at a difficult and frightening moment,” Chief Justice Jill Karofsky said. “We have a responsibility to make that process as clear, accessible, and supportive as possible. This portal is a thoughtful use of technology that keeps petitioners, courts, and community partners at the center.”

     The Wisconsin Court System is one of three courts participating in a national project funded by the U.S. Department of Justice Office on Violence Against Women and administered by the National Center for State Courts. The project focuses on identifying and demonstrating court-based strategies for safe, accessible, fair, and effective virtual civil protection order processes.

     The portal was shaped with technical assistance from Innovation for Justice’s UX for Justice program, which uses user experience research and design methods to evaluate and redesign online court technologies and processes. In Wisconsin, that work included feedback gathering, concept brainstorming, and prototype usability testing.

     “This pilot gives us the opportunity to test the portal in real court settings, learn from the people who use it, and make improvements before statewide launch,” Director of State Courts Audrey K. Skwierawski said. “At its core, this work is about making an urgent court process clearer and more manageable for people seeking protection, while supporting the judges, clerks, advocates, attorneys, and court staff who serve them.”

     Feedback from the pilot sites will be used to refine the portal, which is expected to be made available statewide by the end of August.

     Justice Rebecca Grassl Bradley concludes nearly 11 years of service on the Wisconsin Supreme Court on July 31, completing a judicial career that included service at each of the three levels of Wisconsin’s judiciary.

     She is the first justice in state history to have served as a circuit court judge, a Court of Appeals judge, and a Supreme Court justice.

     Justice Bradley began her judicial career in 2012, when she was appointed to the Milwaukee County Circuit Court. She was elected to the position in 2013. In 2015, Justice Bradley was appointed to the District I Court of Appeals, based in Milwaukee. Later that year, she was appointed to the Wisconsin Supreme Court and sworn in on Oct. 12, 2015.

     She chaired the Supreme Court Legislative Committee as the chief justice’s designee and previously served on the Supreme Court Finance Committee. Her court-system service included membership on the Wisconsin Juvenile Jury Instructions Committee and the Wisconsin Juvenile Benchbook Committee.

     Before joining the bench, Justice Bradley worked in private practice from 1996 to 2012. Her experience included civil and commercial litigation, intellectual property law, and service as vice president of legal operations for a global software company.

     Justice Bradley also served as an arbitrator for the American Arbitration Association and chaired the State Bar of Wisconsin’s Business Law Section.

     Her professional service included membership on the State Bar’s Bench and Bar Committee and prior service on the Wisconsin State Advisory Committee to the U.S. Commission on Civil Rights, the Milwaukee Trial Judges Association, and the Wisconsin Trial Judges Association.

     A Milwaukee native, Justice Bradley graduated from Marquette University in 1993 with an honors bachelor’s degree in business administration and business economics. She earned her law degree from the University of Wisconsin Law School in 1996.

     Justice Bradley’s term ends July 31, concluding nearly 14 years of service on the Wisconsin bench.

7. **First Uniform Bar Examination expands pathway to practice in Wisconsin**
   - Date: 2026-08-05 11:06:39
   - Summary: “Wisconsin administered its first Uniform Bar Examination July 28-29, putting into practice a new approach intended to make it easier for qualified attorneys to begin or continue their careers in the state.”
   - Hero image: **0726ubeexam400.jpg**
   - Hero caption: “Wisconsin's first Uniform Bar Examination was administered July 28 and 29 in Middleton”
   - Body:

     Wisconsin administered its first Uniform Bar Examination July 28-29, putting into practice a new approach intended to make it easier for qualified attorneys to begin or continue their careers in the state.

     A total of 122 applicants registered for the two-day examination. The Wisconsin Supreme Court approved the move to the Uniform Bar Examination, or UBE, in March amid concerns about the availability of attorneys in communities across Wisconsin.

     “Without attorneys the courthouse doors are closed for too many Wisconsinites,” Chief Justice Jill J. Karofsky said. “The UBE addresses the attorney shortage problem head-on by bringing more attorneys into the state. This is a crucial step in ensuring every person in this state has access to justice.”

     The most notable change is the portability of exam scores. An applicant who earns a qualifying UBE score in another participating jurisdiction may use that score to apply for admission in Wisconsin without taking a separate Wisconsin bar exam. Applicants who take the UBE in Wisconsin may likewise transfer their scores when seeking admission elsewhere, subject to each jurisdiction’s requirements.

     To qualify for admission in Wisconsin using a transferred score, an applicant must earn a scaled score of at least 260 and apply within 36 months of taking the qualifying examination. All applicants must also meet Wisconsin’s other admission requirements.

     Wisconsin-specific legal education remains part of the process. Before admission, UBE applicants must complete a 21-hour Wisconsin law and practice educational component. The component must be completed no later than 12 months after notice of a passing score or, for applicants transferring a score from another jurisdiction, within 12 months of applying for admission.

     Developed by the National Conference of Bar Examiners, the two-day UBE includes performance tasks, essays, and multiple-choice questions designed to measure the knowledge and skills considered necessary for entry-level legal practice.

     Wisconsin’s diploma privilege remains unchanged. Eligible graduates of the University of Wisconsin Law School and Marquette University Law School may continue to seek admission to the Wisconsin bar without taking the bar examination.

     More information is available on the Wisconsin Board of Bar Examiners [website](https://www.wicourts.gov/services/attorney/bar.htm).
